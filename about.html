Our story

You donate. We follow.

Earthbux is the social network for charities: a weekly pool that goes where its donors vote, and a newsroom that follows the money.

01 · The gap

Nobody follows the money once it’s given

Most charitable money is warehoused. A private foundation must release only 5% of its assets a year; a donor-advised fund has no payout rule at all. The deduction is taken the day the money leaves — the gift can wait forever.

The money that does move is barely policed. The rules permit generous valuations and creative accounting, and the only account of your donation is written by the people who took it.

02 · The idea

What if giving were an election?

We decide almost everything we share by voting — except where our charity goes. Donors get no options and no control: finding something worth funding is unpaid research, so the winner is whoever asked loudest.

Earthbux makes the donation itself a vote. Together we elect a cause, then the mission inside it, then the organization that runs it — and only then does the money move.

03 · The moment

Attention moved to the feed

Social media took over how people learn, argue and decide. Everything that wants participation now has a network and content behind it — charity still asks the way institutions asked in 1985.

So we built the network and the newsroom into the donation. The research that decides a mission, the posts that build its budget, and the reporting that follows it are the same feed.

People are generous — and hesitant to give without options, control, and a reason to look. An election gives options and control. A newsroom gives feedback and a reason to look. What makes it worth watching is what keeps it honest.

In 2026, the world seemed at risk of total destruction. Earthbux was invented to give us control so we save it.

Our goals

Three things we’re here to do

Goal 1

Give donors control of their money

The weekly pool goes where benefactors vote, not where a grantmaker decides — cause, initiative, organization, every week, for every cause. Until budget day, the part of your stake that isn’t final is still yours to withdraw or move to another mission.

So control is what we hand out: every member is granted 10 tokens in every initiative election — a new one opens each week — a dollar of real say in where the pool goes, whether or not you give a cent of your own.

Goal 2

Hold charities accountable

A slice of every pool funds Earthbux News. Every mission you fund is covered like a story for as long as the money is moving: we report on the cause, and we report on the organization. Money is released in steps against a budget the community approved, and our reporting sits beside the organization’s own.

  1. Journalists

    Publish the mission’s progress live on its page — what was promised, what arrived, what it cost. They travel

  2. Scientists

    Verify the initiatives and vet the organizations, and keep the research scientifically honest. They travel

  3. Auditors

    Track every dollar sent against what the organization said it would spend, and report the difference. Public ledger

This is what a slice of every pool buys: not a receipt, a correspondent.

Goal 3

Make the public more optimistic

Most of what people hear about charity is an appeal or a scandal. We want to show the other part: a mission elected, a budget approved, a receipt, a result — every week, in public, with your name on the vote.

When people can watch their dollar do something, giving stops feeling like a loss and starts feeling like progress. A world that can see itself getting fixed is a world that believes it can be.

Week 15

Mission Prep

What happens

Earthbux orients the organization and its benefactors, and the community builds the budget.

Why

The network here · a public forum budgets the mission

Benefactors post costed budget items and approve them into the budget, and the Analysis — built from the leading Background and Investigation — is elected on budget day. 3/32 of every pool is paid to the winning research and what it cites.

  1. Service

    Labor required. Something we can send people to do — a job, an hourly rate, and the days it takes.

  2. Supply

    Commodities required. What those people need to do it — an item, and what it costs.

  3. Support

    Connections required. An approval, professional help, legal help in a conflict. No cost line: a connection is not a purchase.

  4. Analysis

    Researches the initiative and philanthropy together, and weighs the action plan against comparable programmes. Opens at week 8 · one per mission

What an Earthbuck is

One token is 10¢ of say in one mission

You commit your tokens to the initiative you want. They become that mission’s Earthbucks, and they finalize in steps. Until budget day, the part that isn’t final is still yours to withdraw as cash.

Week 0 · initiative elected10% of your stake is final: deductible, and part of the pool.
Week 8 · organization electedAnother 10% of your organization-election stake is final. Backers of a losing choice can move into another mission.
Week 15 · budget dayEverything is final. You hold the mission’s coin: keep it, or exchange it for a different mission.

How to post

Three kinds of post, one way to make them

Every post is about something. A Background is about a cause; an Investigation is about an organization; a budget item is for an initiative; an Analysis is about a mission — and it is built from the others. A general post can be about any of them, or about nothing at all.

  1. T — the initiative is elected. The most-voted Background is fixed as the mission's leading Background.
  2. T+8 — the organization is elected. The most-voted Investigation of any organization in the race is fixed as its leading Investigation. Analyses open.
  3. T+15 — budget day. The winning Analysis is elected, and the research prize is paid to it and to the research it cites.
  4. After — budget items and research carry on, and any Background or Investigation can be cited again by a later mission.

Every edit is a new version. The version an election used stays exactly as it was. You can bring an old post into a new mission; its votes start again there.
