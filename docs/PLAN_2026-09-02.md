# PART B — the order, and why

## Pass 0 · Decide and unblock — *hours, mostly not code*

1. **Buy the Earthbux email address.** It is the head of the longest chain in the
   whole queue (address → transport → M1–M4 → digest → password reset), it costs
   ten minutes, and nothing else unblocks it.
2. **Write the EBX basis decision into `docs/token_model.md`.** §6 already says
   "what you win is standing, not a rebate" — the basis is a third form of that
   standing and it belongs beside the other two, today, before anything is built
   against the current text.
3. **Answer the seven open questions** in `mission_model.md` §8. Four of them
   (vetting gate, unclaimed donation size, membership tiers, coin timing) are
   read by Passes 3 and 5.
4. Start the **nonprofit-status and banking** enquiries. External lead times are
   the only thing in this document that a good week of building cannot shorten.

*Why first: three of the four are not engineering, they run in parallel with
everything below, and each one is the constraint on a later pass.*

## Pass 1 · Frame the site — *small, wide, low risk*

The five nav tabs, the News rename, the newsroom rename, and the six small
main.html/profile.html fixes.

*Why here: the tab set renames two surfaces (Discussion→News,
Context→Elections). Doing it before the mission page and the newsfeed rebuild
means those two are built once under their final names; doing it after means
touching them twice. Everything in this pass is small and mechanical, so it also
re-familiarises the surfaces before the large passes.*

**Except one:** "still shaky on the election panels" is not actionable as
written. Nothing should be built against it until it names a specific behaviour.

## Pass 2 · Voting bots — *the highest-leverage item in the queue*

> "Need to create voting bots for admin simulation — impossible to test when I'm
> only administrating myself."

*Why this early, and why not filed as housekeeping: every remaining pass ships a
surface that renders an election, a tally, a pool or a mission in flight, and
none of them can be seen in a real state with one account. The mission page in
particular is a page about eight weeks of accumulated activity — building it
against an empty database means building it twice. Bots turn every later pass
from "build and hope" into "build and look at it".*

Scope it small: N synthetic benefactors, weighted random slates and org votes,
posts across all types, run through the real endpoints so the tallies, the
weight curve and the roll are all exercised.

## Pass 3 · The mission page — *the large one, now unblocked*

`docs/mission_model.md` §9 maps the process onto your ASCII layout box by box.

## Pass 5 · The counterparty engine

Org registration/claim page → the four messages → the vetting gates → the check

*Why fifth and not earlier, despite being the heart of phase 3: it is gated on

## Pass 6 · Trust & safety

*Why sixth and not last: it is only ordered here because no real money moves yet.

# PART C — the shape of it

Three of the seven passes (0, 5, and half of 6) are **not engineering**. They are
purchases, external applications, and decisions. The build queue reads as though
it is all code, but the actual critical path runs through an email address, a
bank, and a nonprofit application — and those are the items that will still be
pending in six weeks if they aren't started this week.

The engineering critical path is short and clean: **names (1) → data (2) → the
mission page (3) → the news it feeds (4)**. Everything else can slot around it.
